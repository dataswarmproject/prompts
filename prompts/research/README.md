## Literature Review

### Comprehensive Literature Review

```
Conduct a literature review on [research topic]

Research question: [specific question]
Field: [academic discipline]
Time period: [years to cover]
Scope: [breadth of review]

Requirements:
1. Search strategy:
   - Databases: [PubMed, Google Scholar, etc.]
   - Keywords and search strings
   - Inclusion criteria
   - Exclusion criteria

2. Key themes to explore:
   - [Theme 1]
   - [Theme 2]
   - [Theme 3]

3. Provide:
   - Overview of field
   - Key researchers and institutions
   - Seminal papers (10-15)
   - Recent developments (last 2-3 years)
   - Theoretical frameworks used
   - Methodological approaches
   - Key findings and consensus
   - Gaps in literature
   - Contradictions or debates
   - Future research directions

4. Organize by:
   - [Thematic/Chronological/Methodological]

5. For each key paper include:
   - Authors, year, journal
   - Research question
   - Methodology
   - Key findings
   - Relevance to my research
   - Limitations

6. Synthesis:
   - What we know
   - What we don't know
   - How my research fits

Format: Academic style with proper citations
Citation style: [APA/MLA/Chicago/etc.]
```

### Research Gap Analysis

```
Identify research gaps in [specific topic area]

Current state of knowledge:
[Provide summary of what's known or key papers]

Research context:
- Field: [discipline]
- Sub-field: [specific area]
- Related areas: [connected topics]

Analyze:
1. Current research landscape:
   - Major research streams
   - Dominant theories
   - Common methodologies
   - Typical sample populations

2. What's well-established:
   - Consensus findings
   - Replicated results
   - Strong evidence for [what]

3. Gaps identified:

   Theoretical gaps:
   - [Unexplained phenomena]
   - [Contradictions in theory]

   Methodological gaps:
   - [Understudied methods]
   - [Measurement issues]

   Empirical gaps:
   - [Understudied populations]
   - [Understudied contexts]
   - [Understudied variables]

   Practical gaps:
   - [Implementation knowledge missing]
   - [Real-world application unclear]

4. Research opportunities:
   - High-impact questions to pursue
   - Novel methodological approaches
   - Emerging trends to explore

5. For each significant gap:
   - Why it matters
   - Potential research questions
   - Methodological approach
   - Expected contribution

Prioritize gaps by potential impact and feasibility
```

## Research Design

### Research Proposal

```
Develop a research proposal for [research topic]

Research question: [main question]
Type: [experimental/observational/qualitative/mixed-methods]
Field: [discipline]

Create proposal with:

1. Title:
   - Descriptive and concise

2. Abstract (250 words):
   - Background
   - Research question
   - Method
   - Expected contribution

3. Introduction:
   - Problem statement
   - Significance
   - Context and background
   - Research objectives

4. Literature Review:
   - Theoretical framework
   - Key studies
   - Research gap
   - How this study addresses it

5. Research Questions/Hypotheses:
   - Primary question
   - Secondary questions
   - Hypotheses (if applicable)
   - Operational definitions

6. Methodology:
   - Research design (justify choice)
   - Participants/Sample:
     - Population
     - Sampling method
     - Sample size (with justification)
     - Recruitment strategy
   - Data Collection:
     - Instruments/measures
     - Procedures
     - Timeline
   - Data Analysis:
     - Analytical approach
     - Statistical methods
     - Software tools
   - Validity and reliability measures
   - Ethical considerations

7. Expected Results:
   - Anticipated findings
   - Potential implications
   - Contribution to field

8. Limitations:
   - Potential limitations
   - Mitigation strategies

9. Timeline:
   - Milestones and dates

10. Budget (if needed):
    - Major expense categories

11. References:
    - Key sources cited

Format: [Funding agency/academic institution] requirements
Length: [pages]
```

### Experimental Design

```
Design an experiment to test [hypothesis]

Research question: [question]
Hypothesis: [specific prediction]
Field: [discipline]

Design:
1. Study type:
   - [Between-subjects/within-subjects/mixed]
   - Justification for choice

2. Variables:
   - Independent variable(s): [define and levels]
   - Dependent variable(s): [define and measurement]
   - Control variables: [what to control]
   - Potential confounds: [and how to address]

3. Experimental conditions:
   - Condition 1: [description]
   - Condition 2: [description]
   - Control condition: [description]

4. Participants:
   - Target population
   - Sample size (power analysis)
   - Inclusion/exclusion criteria
   - Recruitment method
   - Random assignment strategy

5. Procedure:
   - Step-by-step protocol
   - Duration
   - Setting
   - Blinding (if applicable)

6. Measurements:
   - How DV measured
   - Measurement instruments
   - Reliability and validity
   - Timing of measurements

7. Data analysis plan:
   - Statistical tests to use
   - Alpha level
   - Planned comparisons
   - How to handle missing data
   - Assumption checks

8. Ethical considerations:
   - IRB requirements
   - Informed consent
   - Risks and benefits
   - Debriefing plan

9. Pilot study:
   - What to test in pilot
   - Sample size for pilot
   - Refinements expected

10. Potential issues:
    - What could go wrong
    - Contingency plans

Provide detailed protocol ready for implementation
```

## Data Collection

### Survey Design

```
Design a survey to measure [construct/behavior]

Research goal: [what you want to learn]
Population: [who you're surveying]
Sample size: [target n]

Create:
1. Survey structure:
   - Introduction and consent
   - Estimated completion time
   - Number of sections
   - Question flow logic

2. Question types:
   - Demographics (which variables)
   - [Main construct 1] (items needed)
   - [Main construct 2] (items needed)
   - Open-ended questions (which topics)

3. For each scale/measure:
   - Construct being measured
   - Number of items
   - Response format (Likert/binary/etc.)
   - Item wording
   - Reverse-coded items
   - Validated scale or custom?

4. Question guidelines:
   - Clear and specific
   - Avoid double-barreled questions
   - Neutral wording (no leading)
   - Appropriate reading level
   - Balanced response options

5. Survey flow:
   - Logical ordering
   - Skip logic needed
   - Randomization strategy
   - Progress indicators

6. Pilot testing plan:
   - Cognitive interviews (n=5)
   - Test survey functionality
   - Time to complete
   - Refinements

7. Administration:
   - Platform: [Qualtrics/Google Forms/etc.]
   - Distribution method
   - Incentive structure
   - Reminder strategy

8. Data quality:
   - Attention checks
   - How to handle incomplete responses
   - How to identify poor quality data

9. Analysis plan:
   - Reliability analysis (Cronbach's alpha)
   - Validity checks
   - Statistical methods

Provide: Complete survey with all questions
```

### Interview Protocol

```
Create an interview protocol for [research purpose]

Research focus: [specific topic]
Interview type: [structured/semi-structured/unstructured]
Participants: [who you're interviewing]
Duration: [minutes]

Develop:
1. Interview guide structure:

   Opening (5 min):
   - Build rapport
   - Explain research
   - Obtain consent
   - Set expectations

   Grand tour questions (10 min):
   - Broad opening questions
   - Establish context
   - [Example question]

   Main topics (30-40 min):

   Topic 1: [theme]
   - Main question
   - Probing questions (3-5)
   - Follow-up areas

   Topic 2: [theme]
   - Main question
   - Probing questions (3-5)
   - Follow-up areas

   [Additional topics]

   Closing (5 min):
   - Summarize key points
   - Anything to add?
   - Thank participant
   - Next steps

2. Question characteristics:
   - Open-ended
   - Non-leading
   - Clear and simple
   - One topic per question

3. Probing strategy:
   - Clarification probes
   - Elaboration probes
   - Example probes
   - Silent probes

4. Interviewer notes:
   - Things to observe
   - Non-verbal cues
   - Memo space

5. Recording:
   - Audio/video setup
   - Backup recording
   - Note-taking approach

6. Ethical considerations:
   - Consent process
   - Right to skip questions
   - Confidentiality assurance
   - Sensitive topic handling

7. Data management:
   - Transcription plan
   - Anonymization strategy
   - Storage and security

Provide: Complete interview protocol ready to use
```

## Analysis

### Qualitative Analysis Plan

```
Create a qualitative analysis plan for [data type]

Study: [brief description]
Data: [interviews/focus groups/observations/documents]
Amount: [number of transcripts/hours]
Approach: [thematic analysis/grounded theory/IPA/etc.]

Analysis plan:

1. Prepare data:
   - Transcription approach
   - Anonymization strategy
   - Data organization

2. Familiarization:
   - Initial read-through process
   - Memo-writing approach

3. Coding strategy:
   - Coding approach: [deductive/inductive/hybrid]
   - Coding unit: [sentence/paragraph/etc.]
   - Initial code development
   - Software: [NVivo/Atlas.ti/MaxQDA/manual]

4. Code development:
   - Initial codes (from theory or data)
   - Coding one transcript
   - Refine codebook
   - Code remaining data
   - Calculate inter-rater reliability (if team)

5. Theme development:
   - Group codes into categories
   - Identify patterns
   - Develop themes
   - Theme hierarchy
   - Theme definitions

6. Verification:
   - Check themes against data
   - Negative case analysis
   - Peer debriefing
   - Member checking (if applicable)

7. Interpretation:
   - What themes mean
   - How themes relate
   - Theoretical connections
   - Answer to research question

8. Reporting:
   - Exemplar quotes selection
   - Theme presentation structure
   - Visual representation (if any)

9. Quality criteria:
   - Credibility measures
   - Transferability
   - Dependability
   - Confirmability

10. Timeline:
    - Each phase duration

Provide: Step-by-step analysis protocol
```

### Statistical Analysis Plan

```
Create a statistical analysis plan for [study]

Study design: [experimental/observational/survey]
Research question: [main question]
Hypotheses: [list hypotheses]

Data:
- Sample size: [n]
- Variables: [list with types]
- Data structure: [independent/repeated measures/nested]

Analysis plan:

1. Descriptive statistics:
   - Variables to describe
   - Measures (mean, SD, frequency, etc.)
   - Tables and figures needed

2. Data screening:
   - Missing data analysis
   - Outlier detection
   - Normality checks
   - Assumption testing

3. Primary analyses:

   Hypothesis 1: [hypothesis]
   - Statistical test: [t-test/ANOVA/regression/etc.]
   - Why this test
   - Variables involved
   - Expected effect size
   - Assumptions to check

   [Repeat for each hypothesis]

4. Secondary analyses:
   - Exploratory analyses
   - Subgroup analyses
   - Sensitivity analyses

5. Effect sizes:
   - Which to report
   - How to interpret

6. Multiple comparison correction:
   - Method: [Bonferroni/FDR/etc.]
   - Adjusted alpha level

7. Missing data:
   - How to handle
   - Imputation method if used
   - Sensitivity analysis

8. Software:
   - [SPSS/R/SAS/Python]
   - Packages needed
   - Code outline

9. Reporting:
   - Tables structure
   - Figures needed
   - Statistics to report
   - APA style guidelines

10. Power analysis:
    - A priori power
    - Sensitivity to show

Provide: Complete analysis protocol with code template
```

## Writing and Publishing

### Academic Paper Outline

```
Create an outline for an academic paper on [research]

Study: [brief description]
Target journal: [journal name]
Word limit: [count]

Outline:

1. Title:
   - Draft title (descriptive, keywords)

2. Abstract (250 words):
   - Background sentence
   - Gap/problem
   - Purpose
   - Method summary
   - Key finding(s)
   - Implication

3. Introduction (750 words):
   - Opening hook
   - Problem statement
   - Literature review summary
   - Research gap
   - Study purpose
   - Research questions/hypotheses
   - Significance
   - [Optional: theoretical framework]

4. Methods (1000-1500 words):
   - Design overview
   - Participants:
     - Recruitment
     - Sample size
     - Demographics
   - Materials/Measures:
     - Each measure described
     - Reliability/validity
   - Procedure:
     - Step by step
   - Analysis plan

5. Results (1000-1500 words):
   - Preliminary analyses
   - Descriptive statistics
   - Main analyses:
     - By hypothesis/research question
     - Statistical results
     - Effect sizes
   - [Tables and figures callouts]

6. Discussion (1500-2000 words):
   - Summary of findings
   - Interpretation (by research question):
     - What it means
     - How it compares to literature
     - Theoretical implications
   - Practical implications
   - Limitations:
     - [Limitation 1 and impact]
     - [Limitation 2 and impact]
   - Future research directions
   - Conclusion (strong ending)

7. References:
   - Key papers to cite

8. Tables:
   - Table 1: [description]
   - Table 2: [description]

9. Figures:
   - Figure 1: [description]
   - Figure 2: [description]

Style: [APA 7th/AMA/Chicago]

Provide: Detailed outline with key points for each section
```

### Grant Proposal

```
Write a grant proposal for [research project]

Grant: [funding source]
Amount requested: [$$$]
Duration: [years]

Required sections:

1. Project Summary (1 page):
   - Problem and significance
   - Specific aims
   - Approach
   - Impact

2. Specific Aims (1 page):
   - Overall goal
   - Aim 1: [description and hypothesis]
   - Aim 2: [description and hypothesis]
   - Aim 3: [description and hypothesis]
   - Expected outcomes
   - Impact statement

3. Research Strategy (6-12 pages):

   Significance:
   - Problem importance
   - Knowledge gaps
   - How this addresses gaps
   - Potential impact

   Innovation:
   - Novel aspects
   - Advancement to field

   Approach:
   For each aim:
   - Rationale
   - Methods (detailed)
   - Expected results
   - Potential problems and solutions
   - Timeline

4. Research Team:
   - PI qualifications
   - Key personnel
   - Collaborators
   - Roles and responsibilities

5. Environment and Resources:
   - Institutional support
   - Facilities
   - Equipment
   - Access to populations/data

6. Budget and Justification:
   - Personnel (with FTE%)
   - Equipment
   - Supplies
   - Travel
   - Other expenses
   - Justify each category

7. Timeline:
   - Gantt chart or milestone table
   - Years 1-X activities

8. Broader Impacts:
   - Scientific impact
   - Societal impact
   - Training impact
   - Dissemination plan

Tone: Compelling but scientific, confident but realistic
Emphasize: Feasibility, significance, innovation

Provide: Complete proposal sections
```
